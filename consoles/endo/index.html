Endometriosis is the most expensive single condition
in every national account that has bothered to count it.

Three countries have now costed endometriosis properly — Australia in 2019, New Zealand in 2025, Finland in 2026. They used different instruments and different populations and they agree on the thing that matters: the bill is mostly not a health-system bill. It is work that never happens.

What the three have in common

Productivity loss is the dominant account in all three, and the range is narrow given how different the methods are: 74.5% of the Finnish total, 75–84% of the Australian, 65–75% of the New Zealand. No health ministry's budget line sees any of it.

The delay layer

€14,618M sits in the eight and a half years before diagnosis

Diagnostic delay is costed separately and deliberately kept out of the annual figure: it is an accumulated stock, not a yearly flow. It is also, by a wide margin, the largest recoverable number attached to this condition anywhere in the model.

Method flag — found while building this console

The delay layer is computed on un-deduplicated indirect cost. The annual figure applies the menstrual-cluster overlap factor J = 0.7935; the delay figure divides it back out. Endometriosis: €1,364.7M annual indirect ÷ 0.7935 × 8.5 years = €14,618.4M. The same is true of adenomyosis, HMB, dysmenorrhea and fibroids, and therefore of the €28,926M national delay total.

This may be deliberate — the delay window predates the diagnoses that overlap — but it is not stated anywhere, and the two layers are not on the same basis. Worth a ruling before the delay number is quoted next to the annual one.

The four things that would replace modelling with measurement

1 · A Kela sickness-allowance code endometriosis can be seen under. 2 · HILMO diagnosis dates, which would replace the age distribution on this page and the 8.5-year delay with measured values. 3 · A Finnish time-to-diagnosis series — the delay number is the weakest input carrying the largest figure. 4 · A presenteeism instrument, in any of the three countries, on any condition.

Method

One engine, three applications

Societal perspective, human-capital approach. Direct costs scale to the full affected population; indirect costs scale only to the employed affected population, by age band. Productivity is decomposed with Strömberg multipliers — 1.97 absenteeism, 1.54 presenteeism, 0.72 applied to the employer-borne increment only — so the construction is identical across jurisdictions and the results can be read against each other.

The lineage is continuous and citable: Strömberg 2017 (the productivity instrument) → Simoens 2012 / WERF EndoCost (the direct-cost instrument) → Tewhaiti-Smith, Gannott et al. 2025 (first application of the Strömberg decomposition to a women's health condition) → the Finnish fifty-condition build, 2026.
